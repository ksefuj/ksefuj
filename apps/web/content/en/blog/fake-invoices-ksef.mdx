An invoice from an unknown company in KSeF (Krajowy System e-Faktur, Poland's National e-Invoicing System) doesn't oblige you to pay or create any tax consequences. Those arise only when you book it, so don't pay, check the seller on the Biała Lista (the VAT taxpayer register) and report the abuse in the Aplikacja Podatnika.

## Why a stranger's invoice lands in your account

KSeF assigns an invoice to the recipient based on the NIP (tax identification number) the issuer enters, and there is no "accept" step on your side. Your NIP is public (CEIDG for sole traders, KRS for companies), so some dishonest actors simply use it. We cover the wider obligation in our [KSeF compendium](/en/blog/ksef-from-april-1-2026).

## Wrong NIP or fraud

**Mistake.** The issuer transposed digits and entered your NIP instead of the right recipient's. Contact the seller and ask for a zeroing correction and a new invoice with the correct NIP. We describe what that correction looks like in the article on the [corrective invoice in KSeF](/en/blog/corrective-invoice-ksef).

**Fraud.** Warning signs (our practical pointers, not an official MF checklist):

- you don't know the company and have never dealt with it,
- the service description means nothing to you, or reads like machine translation,
- the phone rings out and email bounces,
- the payment deadline is absurdly short (three or five days),
- the seller isn't on the Biała Lista (Wykaz Podatników VAT) or its status is "inactive".

## What to do, step by step

### 1. Don't pay and don't book it

An invoice visible in KSeF doesn't oblige you to pay or to deduct VAT. Under Art. 86(10) of the VAT Act, the right to deduct arises in the period in which the invoice is entered in your records, not when it appears in KSeF. Art. 88(3a)(4) excludes deducting VAT on invoices documenting transactions that never took place.

### 2. Check the seller on the Biała Lista

Enter the NIP from the invoice in the [Wykaz Podatników VAT](https://www.podatki.gov.pl/wykaz-podatnikow-vat-wyszukiwarka). An active entry with details matching the invoice suggests an honest mistake, so get in touch. No entry, an "inactive" status or mismatched details is a strong sign of fraud: don't contact the seller and go to step 3.

### 3. Report the abuse in the Aplikacja Podatnika

Since 1 April 2026 the Aplikacja Podatnika KSeF (the taxpayer portal) has a simplified way to report suspicious invoices, with no letters to the tax office:

1. Log in at [ap.ksef.mf.gov.pl](https://ap.ksef.mf.gov.pl/).
2. Find the invoice in your inbox.
3. Use the "report abuse" option on the document.
4. Add a short reason, for example "I don't know this company and I didn't order this service".

According to the KSeF 2.0 Manual and the MF FAQ:

- **one report covers one invoice**, so three suspicious invoices mean three reports,
- **only the buyer named on the invoice can report**,
- **KAS (the National Revenue Administration) reviews reports but doesn't tell you the outcome**,
- the report is a technical tool of the application; from the MF information available, the VAT Act doesn't regulate it as a separate procedure,
- **you can withdraw a report** if the seller gets in touch and issues a correction.

<Tip>
  A report in the app doesn't replace a complaint to law enforcement. If you suspect a crime,
  consult a lawyer or tax adviser.
</Tip>

### 4. Tell your accountant

Your accounting firm sees your invoices but doesn't know your contracts. With automatic KSeF import, a suspicious invoice can be booked in routine work, especially at month-end. Tell them plainly: "This invoice is suspicious, please don't book it until this is resolved". If it already reached the records, ask them to remove it before the period closes, and ask whether their software can flag invoices from unknown counterparties before import.

## Reducing the risk

Nothing fully stops someone entering your NIP on a fake invoice. Two habits help: check KSeF every few days instead of once a month, and agree with your accountant that an invoice from a new company needs your confirmation first.

## Phishing that impersonates KSeF

Since [mandatory KSeF went live](/en/blog/ksef-launched), emails impersonating the Ministry of Finance or KSeF have circulated, with "login" links and requests for your details. Don't click links in emails about KSeF. Go to [ksef.podatki.gov.pl](https://ksef.podatki.gov.pl/) by typing it or from a bookmark; official notifications never require you to click a confirmation link.

If the stranger's invoice turned out to be a plain NIP mistake, the next step is the [zeroing correction](/en/blog/corrective-invoice-ksef#zero-value-correction-when-the-buyers-nip-is-wrong).
