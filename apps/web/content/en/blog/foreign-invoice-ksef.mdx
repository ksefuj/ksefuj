An invoice to a foreign buyer goes through KSeF (Poland's National e-Invoicing System) just like a domestic one, even if the buyer has no Polish NIP and will never log in. In the buyer's data you never put a foreign number in the NIP field: for EU companies use `KodUE` + `NrVatUE`, for everyone else `KodKraju` + `NrID`, and when there is no number, `BrakID`.

The buyer won't receive the invoice from KSeF, so send them a readable copy (a PDF with the QR code) by email or post. Below: the fields for each buyer type, VAT rates and annotations. Since [1 April 2026](/en/blog/ksef-from-april-1-2026) every taxpayer issues invoices in KSeF, VAT-registered and VAT-exempt alike (the largest companies since 1 February). Until the end of 2026 you may still issue an invoice outside KSeF if your invoiced sales in that month stay at or below [10,000 PLN gross](/en/blog/ksef-10000-pln-limit).

## EU buyer with a VAT-EU number

In the Podmiot2 block (the buyer) use the pair `KodUE` + `NrVatUE`, **not NIP**. A foreign number in the NIP field gets the invoice rejected.

- **`KodUE`**: two-letter EU country prefix (`DE`, `NL`, `FR`)
- **`NrVatUE`**: the VAT number **without the country prefix** (`123456789`, not `DE123456789`)
- **`Nazwa`**: the buyer's full company name

In the address give `KodKraju` (the same code as in `KodUE`; required), `AdresL1` (street and number) and `AdresL2` (postcode and city).

<Tip>
  Check the number in the European Commission's VIES system before issuing the invoice. An inactive
  number causes problems with settling WDT (intra-EU supply of goods) or intra-EU services.
</Tip>

Example, an invoice to a German company:

<XmlExample copyable>
  ```xml
  <Podmiot2>
    <DaneIdentyfikacyjne>
      <KodUE>DE</KodUE>
      <NrVatUE>123456789</NrVatUE>
      <Nazwa>Berlin Design GmbH</Nazwa>
    </DaneIdentyfikacyjne>
    <Adres>
      <KodKraju>DE</KodKraju>
      <AdresL1>Musterstraße 1</AdresL1>
      <AdresL2>10115 Berlin</AdresL2>
    </Adres>
    <JST>2</JST>
    <GV>2</GV>
  </Podmiot2>
  ```
</XmlExample>

`JST` and `GV` are mandatory in every Podmiot2 block. For foreign buyers enter `2` ("not applicable"), otherwise the invoice fails validation.

<Source href="https://ec.europa.eu/taxation_customs/vies/" label="VIES — VAT number verification" />

## EU buyer without a VAT number

A private individual, a freelancer on a flat-rate scheme or a company not registered for VAT has no number to enter. Instead of `KodUE` + `NrVatUE` use `BrakID` = `1` (the buyer has no identification number). `Nazwa` stays mandatory. An invoice for a private person (a consumer) doesn't have to go through KSeF (art. 106ga(2)(4) of the VAT Act); you may issue it there voluntarily, see our post on [invoices for private persons](/en/blog/invoice-for-private-person-ksef).

<XmlExample copyable>
  ```xml
  <Podmiot2>
    <DaneIdentyfikacyjne>
      <BrakID>1</BrakID>
      <Nazwa>Anna Müller</Nazwa>
    </DaneIdentyfikacyjne>
    <Adres>
      <KodKraju>DE</KodKraju>
      <AdresL1>Hauptstraße 5</AdresL1>
      <AdresL2>80331 München</AdresL2>
    </Adres>
    <JST>2</JST>
    <GV>2</GV>
  </Podmiot2>
  ```
</XmlExample>

<Warning>
  Without a VAT-EU number you can't apply the `0 WDT` rate to goods or `np II` to intra-EU services.
  Taxation rules may differ in that case, so discuss it with your accountant.
</Warning>

## Non-EU buyer

A client from the USA, Switzerland, Canada or the UK (not in the EU since Brexit) has no `KodUE` or `NrVatUE`. You have two options.

**The buyer has a foreign tax identifier.** Use `KodKraju` + `NrID` in the `DaneIdentyfikacyjne` block. They are two separate sibling elements, not nested.

- **`KodKraju`**: ISO country code (`US`, `GB`, `CH`, `NO`)
- **`NrID`**: the foreign identification number (EIN in the US, UTR in the UK, UID in Switzerland)

`KodKraju` appears twice: in `DaneIdentyfikacyjne` (tax identification) and in `Adres` (registered address). Both are required.

<XmlExample copyable>
  ```xml
  <Podmiot2>
    <DaneIdentyfikacyjne>
      <KodKraju>US</KodKraju>
      <NrID>12-3456789</NrID>
      <Nazwa>US Design Inc.</Nazwa>
    </DaneIdentyfikacyjne>
    <Adres>
      <KodKraju>US</KodKraju>
      <AdresL1>123 Main Street</AdresL1>
      <AdresL2>New York, NY 10001</AdresL2>
    </Adres>
    <JST>2</JST>
    <GV>2</GV>
  </Podmiot2>
  ```
</XmlExample>

**The buyer has no identifier.** As with an EU buyer without a number, use `BrakID` = `1`:

<XmlExample copyable>
  ```xml
  <DaneIdentyfikacyjne>
    <BrakID>1</BrakID>
    <Nazwa>John Smith Freelance</Nazwa>
  </DaneIdentyfikacyjne>
  ```
</XmlExample>

## VAT rates on foreign invoices

The rate depends on **what** you sell (goods or services) and **to whom** (EU or non-EU buyer).

| Transaction type              | Rate (P_12) | Net amount field | When to use                                                                     |
| :---------------------------- | :---------- | :--------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| WDT — goods to the EU         | `0 WDT`     | `P_13_6_2`       | Goods sold to a buyer with a VAT-EU number                                      |
| Export of goods               | `0 EX`      | `P_13_6_3`       | Goods sold to a non-EU buyer                                                    |
| Services to EU businesses     | `np II`     | `P_13_9`         | Services under Art. 100(1)(4) of the VAT Act (Art. 28b, B2B to an EU taxpayer)  |
| Services to non-EU businesses | `np I`      | `P_13_8`         | Supply of goods or services outside Poland, other than `np II` services and OSS |

<Source
  href="https://ksef.podatki.gov.pl/media/nuclnu0w/broszura-informacyjna-dotyczaca-struktury-logicznej-fa-3.pdf"
  label="FA(3) information brochure, p. 92: np I and np II rates"
/>

In practice: a website built for a German client with a VAT-EU number is a service, rate `np II`. The same website for a US client is `np I`. Goods shipped to a Dutch company with a VAT-EU number are a WDT (`0 WDT`), and goods shipped to a UK client are an export (`0 EX`).

<Warning>
  The `oo` rate covers domestic reverse charge only. For sales abroad use `np I`, `np II`, `0 WDT`
  or `0 EX`. KSeF won't catch this mistake: a file with `oo` passes validation, but the rate is
  wrong.
</Warning>

Rate values in FA(3) are case- and space-sensitive. Correct: `np I`, `np II`, `0 WDT`, `0 EX`. Incorrect: `NP I`, `npI`, `0WDT`.

## Annotations: P_18 and P_18A

All eight fields of the Adnotacje block are mandatory, even when the value is "not applicable". Two of them are easy to mix up and both matter for foreign invoices:

| Field   | Value | Meaning                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| :------ | :---- | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `P_18`  | `1`   | Reverse charge: the buyer accounts for VAT or a similar tax under the place-of-taxation rules. The `np I` or `np II` rate alone does not decide this                                      |
| `P_18`  | `2`   | All other cases, e.g. a supply or service at `np I` where you account for the foreign tax yourself. For WDT (`0 WDT`) and export (`0 EX`), MF sample invoices 22 and 23 have `P_18` = `2` |
| `P_18A` | `1`   | Split payment (MPP): total amount due over PLN 15,000 (or the equivalent in a foreign currency), goods or services from Annex 15 to the VAT Act, sold to a taxpayer                       |
| `P_18A` | `2`   | No MPP: at least one of the three conditions in the row above is not met                                                                                                                  |

`P_18` marks reverse charge and `P_18A` marks [split payment](/en/blog/split-payment-mpp-ksef). They are two independent fields, each with its own condition. What decides `P_18` is who accounts for the tax, not the `np` rate; for services at `np II` that depends on the place-of-supply rules. If you don't know who accounts for the tax abroad, ask your accountant or tax adviser. The FA(3) schema does not exclude WDT, exports or foreign buyers from MPP. You enter `P_18A` = `2` when any condition is missing, for example when nothing on the invoice is from Annex 15, or when the total amount due, converted to PLN the same way as the taxable amount, does not exceed PLN 15,000. The MF materials we found don't settle whether a foreign buyer meets the "sold to a taxpayer" condition, so it is unclear whether you enter `1` for Annex 15 goods sold above the threshold as WDT or export, or for Annex 15 services supplied to a foreign company. We describe the full Adnotacje structure in the article on [the most common FA(3) validation errors](/en/blog/most-common-fa3-validation-errors).

## Invoices in a foreign currency

You can issue the invoice in any currency (EUR, USD, GBP, CHF); `KodWaluty` takes ISO 4217 codes, and you give net and gross amounts in that currency. One exception: the **VAT amount must be in PLN**, converted at the [NBP average rate](/en/waluty) from the last business day before the tax point. At `np I`, `np II`, `0 WDT` and `0 EX` the VAT is zero, so no conversion is needed.

If you're unsure about the buyer fields in your XML file, check them in the [ksefuj.to validator](/en/validator) before sending to KSeF. The file never leaves your browser.
