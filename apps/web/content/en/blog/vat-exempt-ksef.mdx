VAT exemption does not exempt you from KSeF (Krajowy System e-Faktur, Poland's national e-invoicing system). Every invoice you issue to another business goes through KSeF, just as it does for VAT-registered taxpayers. Until the end of 2026 you can still stay outside it if your invoices total no more than 10,000 zł gross in a month, and from 1 January 2027 there is no limit.

Legal status as of 7 October 2026.

## Do you have to issue an invoice at all

No. Art. 106b ust. 2 of the VAT Act removes the duty to issue invoices for sales exempt under art. 113 ust. 1 (since 2026 the sales limit is 240,000 zł) or art. 43 ust. 1. If the buyer asks for one, though, you must issue it, as long as the request comes within 3 months of the end of the month in which the service was performed or payment received (art. 106b ust. 3 pkt 2). The invoice states the legal basis of the exemption, for example art. 113 ust. 1 of the VAT Act (art. 106e ust. 1 pkt 19).

A „rachunek” (simple bill) is not an alternative. In its FAQ the Ministry of Finance (MF) says that exempt transactions are documented with „fakturą wystawianą na żądanie nabywcy, a nie rachunkiem” (an invoice issued on the buyer's request, not a bill), and that an invoice an exempt taxpayer issues to another taxpayer goes through KSeF. The answer was given to a budget unit exempt from VAT. MF has not published a separate position for sole traders.

Example: Ewa runs a hair salon and uses the art. 113 exemption. Her clients pay on the spot and do not want invoices. In October 2026 an events agency books styling for a photo shoot and asks for an invoice for 3,200 zł. Ewa issues it, because the agency is a business and asked for it.

## From when the invoice goes through KSeF

Art. 106ga ust. 1 says that taxpayers must issue structured invoices using KSeF, and in its FAQ on art. 145m MF states outright that invoices from exempt taxpayers to other taxpayers are issued in KSeF. Ewa therefore has the same dates as everyone else: [KSeF has applied since 1 April 2026](/en/blog/ksef-from-april-1-2026), with a temporary exception for small sales.

The exception is art. 145m, the [10,000 zł limit](/en/blog/ksef-10000-pln-limit). Until 31 December 2026 you may issue electronic or paper invoices outside KSeF if their total gross value in a given month is at most 10,000 zł. The month the invoice is issued counts. Invoices to private individuals and cash-register invoices do not count towards the limit, and the invoice that crosses it, and every one after it, goes through KSeF (ust. 2).

In December Ewa issues an invoice for 4,500 zł to the agency and one for 6,000 zł to a producer. The first fits within the limit. The second brings the total to 10,500 zł, so it must go through KSeF.

From 1 January 2027 the exception expires. MF's page on the implementation stages says KSeF becomes mandatory for „wcześniej zwolnionych przedsiębiorców (do 10 tys. zł miesięcznie)” (businesses previously exempted, up to 10,000 zł a month). That is an exemption from KSeF based on the limit, not a VAT exemption, but it applies to you in the same way.

There is one more case. Suppose the agency asks for an invoice for the October service only on 15 January 2027 (the 3-month window ends on 31 January). Art. 145m no longer applies, because it covers the period up to 31 December 2026. By the wording of the provision, that invoice goes through KSeF. MF has not addressed this case directly.

Until the end of 2026, cash-register invoices and fiscal receipts with a NIP up to 450 zł can also stay outside KSeF (art. 145n). From 2027 an invoice to a business for a sale rung up on a cash register must go through KSeF too.

<Warning>
  From 1 January 2027, art. 106ni provides a penalty for an invoice issued outside KSeF: up to 100%
  of the tax on the invoice, or, where the invoice shows no tax, up to 18.7% of the total amount
  due. For a VAT-exempt seller the second rate applies. MF has announced a deferral of penalties to
  the end of 2027, but the draft (UD477) is not yet law, and MF itself stresses that the deferral
  does not remove the duty to use KSeF.
</Warning>

## An invoice for a private individual

Invoices to individuals who do not run a business do not require KSeF (art. 106ga ust. 2 pkt 4). You issue them on the customer's request, as above, on paper or electronically. MF also allows sending such an invoice to KSeF voluntarily; the customer then receives it in an agreed way, for example as a PDF.

## The art. 113a exception is not you

Taxpayers under art. 106ga ust. 2 pkt 6, that is, those using the art. 113a exemption, are permanently outside KSeF. It is an exemption for businesses seated in another EU member state that sell in Poland with annual EU turnover up to 100,000 euro. A Polish business on the art. 113 exemption is not in that group.

## How to start without paid software

Aplikacja Podatnika KSeF 2.0 is free, and MF describes it as a full alternative to commercial invoicing software. You need a NIP, because KSeF cannot issue an invoice without the seller's NIP.

As a sole trader you get owner permissions automatically, with no application. You log in at [ap.ksef.mf.gov.pl](https://ap.ksef.mf.gov.pl/) with a Profil Zaufany (Polish trusted profile) or a qualified electronic signature. The ZAW-FA form is needed only when the signature contains neither a NIP nor a PESEL. We describe the difference between certificates and tokens in [our post on certificates and tokens](/en/blog/certificates-vs-tokens-ksef), but you need neither to issue invoices by hand after logging in with a Profil Zaufany.
